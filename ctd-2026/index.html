Research Question

How can we design flexible RL agents that can sample-efficiently adapt their behavior to solve any task, where each task may involve multiple conflicting objectives?

  • Flexible
    • Identify the most appropriate behavior for any unseen task
  • Sample-efficient
    • Solve tasks with as few interactions with the environment as possible
  • Adaptive
    • Adjust its policy according to the preferences over the objectives

Generalized Policy Evaluation (GPE)

directly evaluates how well a policy \pi would perform

under any reward function r_{\boldsymbol{w}} (without having to deploy it!):

q^{\textcolor{#0070C0}{\pi}}_{\textcolor{#ED7D31}{\boldsymbol{w}}}(s,a) = \psi^{\textcolor{#0070C0}{\pi}}(s,a) \cdot \textcolor{#ED7D31}{\boldsymbol{w}}

Multi-Objective RL

Convex Coverage Set (CCS)

\text{CCS} \triangleq \{\mathbf{v}^{\pi} \in \mathcal{F} \mid \exists\, \mathbf{w} \text{ s.t. } \forall \mathbf{v}^{\pi'} \in \mathcal{F},\ \mathbf{v}^{\pi} \cdot \mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{v}^{\pi'} \cdot \mathbf{w}\}

The optimal policy for any

linear combination of rewards

is in the CCS!

GPI Prioritization

[Contrib. 2]

GPI-based prioritization to improve sample efficiency in MORL:

GPI Linear Support (GPI-LS)

  • Identifies the most promising preferences/objectives to train on
  • Guaranteed to identify optimal (or ε-optimal) sets of policies

GPI Prioritized Dyna (GPI-PD)

  • Identifies relevant past experiences to learn a new policy
  • First model-based MORL method for continuous states/actions
